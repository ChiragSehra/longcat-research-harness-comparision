# Decision memo: India’s 2024 election and the durability of political change

**Evidence cutoff:** 11 October 2026, 23:59:59 Asia/Kolkata.  
**Decision:** Treat 2024 as a consequential correction within a BJP-centred political system, with possible regional realignments and more contested governing authority. Do not yet classify it as a durable national transfer of voter allegiance.

## Executive conclusion

The strongest interpretation is **electoral resilience combined with conditional dominance**. The BJP lost its independent Lok Sabha majority in 2024, but retained a large national vote base. Subsequent victories in Haryana, Maharashtra, Delhi, Bihar, Assam and West Bengal make an enduring national swing toward the opposition difficult to sustain. Conversely, opposition successes in Jharkhand and Kerala, Tamil Nadu’s emergence of a new regional challenger, and a defeated constitutional amendment show that neither electoral competition nor parliamentary constraints disappeared. The facts supporting this judgment are set out below; the judgment itself is an inference, not an official finding.

“Realignment” should require repeated changes in social allegiance, party competition and coalition organisation that survive different contests. A lost majority is insufficient. Equally, calling 2024 a one-off must not imply that its institutional consequences were imaginary. My confidence is moderate: later state contests test durability, but cannot substitute for another national election.

For decisions about political risk, use three separate assumptions: the BJP remains the main organising pole; national policy depends on the coalition and the kind of parliamentary majority required; regional outcomes and voter blocs remain capable of breaking national narratives. Avoid projecting either the opposition’s June 2024 momentum or the BJP’s subsequent recoveries mechanically into the next general election.

## What changed in 2024?

The vote–seat distinction is essential. Using the government’s published comparison, BJP support declined by **1.14 percentage points**, while its seats fell by 63, or **20.8%**; those are calculations from the cited figures. BJP’s 240 seats left it 32 short of the 272-seat majority threshold in a 543-seat House. This is substantial punishment, but not electoral collapse. Congress’s 99 seats and 21.19% vote share improved its capacity to oppose the government without putting it near the BJP’s national electoral scale. [2019 government table](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/userfiles/GE2024/files/basic-html/page67.html), [2024 government summary, printed page 9](https://newindiasamachar.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/Magazine/2024/Jun/M202406161.pdf), [Lokniti tables, PDF pages 1–2](https://www.lokniti.org/media/PDF-upload/1755059632_52889800_party_performance.pdf)

National shares also conceal changes in where parties stood and whom allies represented. Congress contested 328 seats and the BJP 441 in Lokniti’s compilation. A party’s nationwide percentage therefore mixes support in contested constituencies with alliance decisions to leave constituencies to partners. A higher percentage does not, by itself, identify individual voters switching parties. [Lokniti party performance](https://www.lokniti.org/media/PDF-upload/1755059632_52889800_party_performance.pdf)

Uttar Pradesh supplied the clearest evidence of a potentially meaningful regional change: the SP–Congress partnership won 43 seats, against the BJP’s 33. Coordination offered an alternative capable of converting support into victories. My inference is that candidate selection, social mobilisation and fewer competing opposition candidates can amplify a modest aggregate shift; separating their effects would require constituency-level counterfactual analysis. [Final UP results](https://indianexpress.com/elections/2024-uttar-pradesh-lok-sabha-election-results-full-list-of-winners-on-all-80-seats-of-uttar-pradesh-9365613/)

Coalition arithmetic changed immediately. TDP won 16 seats and JD(U) 12; the contemporary NDA support figure was 293 MPs. Subtracting both parties gives 265, whereas subtracting either alone leaves more than 272. Thus their combined importance should not be confused with each possessing an automatic, unilateral government-toppling veto. These are formation-time figures, not a certified October 2026 whip count. [Lokniti party results](https://www.lokniti.org/media/PDF-upload/1755059632_52889800_party_performance.pdf), [contemporary NDA total, printed pages 1–2](https://assets.carnegieendowment.org/static/files/Vaishnav%20Mallory%20-%20Reassessing%204th%20Party%20System.pdf)

## The strongest case for a one-off correction

The best case rests on repeated recovery rather than assertions that voters misunderstood the national campaign. Haryana returned a BJP majority in October 2024. Maharashtra’s three principal ruling partners then won 230 seats combined, a calculation from their final tallies. Delhi followed in February 2025, and Bihar’s NDA won 202 seats in November. These outcomes are incompatible with a simple story of uniformly accelerating rejection of BJP-led rule. [Haryana](https://indianexpress.com/elections/haryana-elections-results-full-list-of-winners-9607858/), [Maharashtra](https://newsonair.gov.in/bjp-led-mahayuti-alliance-sweeps-assembly-elections-in-maharashtra-jmm-led-india-bloc-retains-power-in-jharkhand/), [Delhi](https://www.adrindia.org/sites/default/files/English_Analysis_of_Funds_Collected_Expenditure_Incurred_Political_Parties_Delhi_Election_2025.pdf), [Bihar](https://halifax.citynews.ca/2025/11/14/modis-coalition-leads-in-a-key-indian-state-election/)

The 2026 evidence is stronger still. Assam’s BJP majority and the conquest of West Bengal demonstrate substantial capacity to retain and expand power. Bengal’s final election tally must include Falta’s later poll: the widely circulated initial count of 207 BJP seats covered 293 declared constituencies; the completed tally was 208. [ECI Assam](https://results.eci.gov.in/ResultAcGenMay2026/partywiseresult-S03.htm), [ADR initial Bengal tally](https://www.adrindia.org/sites/default/files/West_Bengal_Assembly_Elections_2026_Criminal_and_Financial_background_details_of_Winning_Candidates_FinalVer_Eng_0.pdf), [Falta completion](https://www.newindianexpress.com/states/west-bengal/2026/May/24/counting-underway-for-falta-assembly-repoll-in-bengal-bjp-takes-commanding-lead-after-five-round)

Named expert analysis supports a distinction between weakened dominance and a new party system. Milan Vaishnav and Caroline Mallory argued in September 2024 that the BJP remained the defining pole around which parties aligned, despite losing its majority. Later recoveries are consistent with that interpretation, though they do not validate every aspect of their argument. [Carnegie paper](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2024/09/india-election-bjp-party-politics)

The important qualification is that state victories cannot retrospectively erase the national verdict. They test the broad backlash hypothesis; they do not establish that precisely the same voters, choosing between identical alternatives, returned to the BJP.

## The strongest case for durable realignment

The strongest alternative concerns **social coalitions and the agenda**, rather than an inevitable opposition government. Lokniti survey evidence discussed by Vaishnav and Mallory shows the BJP-led alliance losing ground among non-Yadav OBCs and non-Jatav Dalits in Uttar Pradesh. Its estimated support among the latter declined from 48% to 29%; Brahmin support remained high, at 79% versus 82% in 2019. Muslim voters increasingly coordinated around the strongest anti-BJP challenger; the reported UP SP–Congress estimate was 92%. These are survey estimates, not caste counts from electoral ballots. [Carnegie PDF, printed pages 27–28, figure 17](https://assets.carnegieendowment.org/static/files/Vaishnav%20Mallory%20-%20Reassessing%204th%20Party%20System.pdf)

Such changes would become durable if broader caste coalitions survived beyond a single national campaign. The opposition also placed representation on the policy agenda: Congress’s 2024 manifesto promised a socioeconomic caste census and an amendment raising the reservation ceiling. The government subsequently approved caste enumeration in the forthcoming census in April 2025; an April 2026 government publication identified it with Census 2027. That verifies policy adaptation, not that the election alone caused it or that Congress’s full programme was adopted. [Congress manifesto](https://manifesto.inc.in/en/equity/), [Cabinet announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2125526&lang=1&reg=1), [2026 census backgrounder](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2255461&lang=1&reg=3)

Regional alternatives also remained substantial. Jharkhand resisted the BJP-led challenge in 2024, and Kerala’s 2026 result favoured Congress rather than BJP. Tamil Nadu delivered its largest party tally to TVK, with 108 seats, below an outright majority. The analytical lesson is that dissatisfaction can reorganise regional competition without flowing to either national camp. [Jharkhand results](https://newsonair.gov.in/bjp-led-mahayuti-alliance-sweeps-assembly-elections-in-maharashtra-jmm-led-india-bloc-retains-power-in-jharkhand/), [Kerala official summary](https://www.ceo.kerala.gov.in/ceokerala/pdf/govt_orders/legislative-assembly-2026/statistical-report/2-List_of_Successful_Candidates_1778164525.pdf), [ECI Tamil Nadu](https://results.eci.gov.in/ResultAcGenMay2026/partywiseresult-S22.htm)

This is a serious case for greater contestability and some regional realignments. It is weaker as evidence of a settled, nationwide replacement of the BJP’s electoral coalition.

## Major voter blocs: mixed motives, limited causal identification

Material dissatisfaction and cultural approval coexisted. In Lokniti’s national post-poll survey, 23.1% named unemployment and 23.8% inflation as the government’s most disliked work; 22.4% named Ram Mandir construction as its most liked work. These questions measure reported evaluations, not the proportion voting because of each issue. The BJP manifesto promised continued free ration and expanded senior-citizen health coverage, demonstrating an appeal extending beyond cultural mobilisation. [Survey, questions 11a–11b](https://lokniti.org/media/PDF-upload/1718435207_67606300_download_report.pdf), [BJP manifesto document](https://www.scobserver.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/2024-BJP-Election-Manifest-English.pdf)

Women and farmers should likewise not be treated as fixed national vote banks. Rajeshwari Deshpande’s Maharashtra post-poll analysis reported only a three-point ruling-alliance advantage among women over men and cautioned against attributing the victory principally to women. The same compilation found agrarian distress alongside considerable farmer support for Mahayuti and uneven associations between welfare receipt and voting. These findings favour multiple explanations over a single welfare mechanism. [Lokniti’s original newspaper compilation, PDF pages 2–3](https://lokniti.org/media/upload_files/Lokniti-CSDS%20Maharashtra.pdf)

For Adivasis, Jharkhand is important counterevidence to national homogenisation. Suhas Palshikar’s post-poll account emphasised Jharkhandi identity and Adivasi culture, with limited rewards from the BJP’s Hindutva campaign. The inference is that caste, tribe, religion, gender and livelihood intersect with local party competition; an aggregate bloc label is not a durable allegiance. [Lokniti Jharkhand analysis](https://lokniti.org/media/upload_files/Jharkhand%20Articles%20%281%29.pdf)

## Governing constraints through the cutoff

Coalition accommodation was visible in the July 2024 Budget: it promised to arrange ₹15,000 crore for Andhra Pradesh through multilateral agencies and announced Bihar infrastructure support. These are announcements, not proof of expenditure or a documented bargaining exchange. [Budget speech, paragraphs 30–36](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/budget2024-25/doc/Budget_Speech.pdf)

The government nevertheless passed the Waqf amendment in April 2025. In contrast, its April 2026 constitutional amendment concerning delimitation failed, with 298 votes for and 230 against: sufficient for an ordinary majority, insufficient for the required special majority. This illustrates selective constraints rather than general paralysis. [PRS April 2025 review](https://prsindia.org/policy/monthly-policy-review/april-2025), [public broadcaster’s division report](https://newsonair.gov.in/hi/131st-constitutional-amendment-bill-not-passed-lok-sabha-vote-count/)

Subsequently, PRS recorded 12 Bills passed during the July–August 2026 Monsoon Session, while the simultaneous-elections constitutional amendment remained with a joint committee. September’s delimitation debate still exposed disagreement over southern representation. [PRS session wrap](https://prsindia.org/sessiontrack/monsoon-session-2026/session-wrap), [September 29 debate](https://www.indiatoday.in/india/story/delimitation-federalism-debate-south-parties-warn-on-voice-bjp-backs-process-3005847-2026-09-29)

Coalition leverage was also evolving. June 2026 reporting recorded 20 Trinamool MPs claiming a separate bloc supporting the NDA and a subsequent announced merger with NCPI. Pratul Sharma cautioned that acquiring MPs did not automatically confer a new electorate or organisation. I treat these as reported elite movements, without certifying their final legal recognition or an October membership total. [PTI account](https://theprint.in/india/after-assembly-revolt-tmc-split-reaches-lok-sabha-as-20-mps-form-separate-bloc-back-nda/2954472/), [merger announcement](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/trinamool-rebel-bloc-to-merge-with-nationalist-citizens-party-kakoli-ghosh-11635562/), [Sharma analysis](https://www.theweek.in/voices/columns/pratul-sharma/2026/06/19/anti-defection-law-tenth-schedule-coalition-politics-parliament-monsoon-session.html)

## Uncertainties and limits

First, no second national election establishes whether 2024’s social shifts persisted. State elections differ in candidates, alliances and governing responsibility. Seat-share comparisons exaggerate small swings; neither opposition coordination nor subsequent recoveries identify individual switching.

Second, survey subgroup estimates carry sampling and recall uncertainty. Lokniti’s 2024 study covered 19,663 respondents, 23 states and 191 constituencies; it is not a longitudinal panel tracking the same voters into 2026. [Study metadata](https://www.lokniti.org/national-election-studies)

Third, Bengal’s electoral-roll controversy complicates causal interpretation. Debraj Ray’s May 2026 analysis concluded that deletions alone would overturn the BJP majority only under extreme assumptions about excluded voters’ turnout and preferences. That is a sensitivity analysis, not proof that every deletion was justified or harmless. [Ray’s original paper](https://debrajray.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/SIR2026.pdf)

Fourth, several ECI pages failed direct extraction. Accessible official tables, indexed ECI text, government publications and attributed secondary reports were used transparently; no complete Form-20 reconstruction or certified cutoff-date parliamentary roster was obtained. Later publication or crawl dates do not extend the evidence cutoff. The accompanying trail records accesses, discrepancies and calculations.

The decision should change toward national realignment only when repeat national evidence shows persistent social switching and effective opposition coordination. Until then, plan for resilient BJP leadership with variable regional competition and issue-specific limits on its authority.
