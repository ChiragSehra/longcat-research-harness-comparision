### S1.1｜Decision and operational tests of durability

**The strongest supported answer is a consequential correction with institutional effects; durable national voter realignment remains unproved.** Confidence is moderate about the 2024 change, but low about its persistence through 11 October 2026 because comparable subsequent electoral and survey evidence was not recovered.

The decisive distinction is BJP versus NDA: BJP won 240 seats, below the 272 majority threshold, while NDA secured 293. Congress’s 99 seats likewise should not be conflated with INDIA’s broader strength. These results established dependence on partners at government formation without displacing BJP as the largest party. [PRS’s election profile](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/profile-of-the-18th-lok-sabha) corroborates party seats; [Vaishnav and Mallory’s September 2024 analysis](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2024/09/india-election-bjp-party-politics) supplies alliance totals and the strongest continuity argument: BJP remained the organizing pole of competition.

Governing evidence supports a qualified constraint interpretation. PRS records [Waqf legislation passing both chambers in April 2025](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-waqf-amendment-bill-2024), but [the constitutional delimitation bill being negatived on 17 April 2026](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-constitution-131st-amendment-bill-2026). This contrast suggests continuing legislative capacity alongside limits on constitutional change; it does not establish that allies caused the defeat.

Neither a *one-off* correction nor durable regional or voter-bloc realignment is demonstrated by the recovered evidence. Missing comparable returns and weighted survey findings prevent convergence tests. The mixed interpretation would strengthen if regional gains and bloc shifts recur alongside surviving alliances and documented bargaining concessions; broad electoral reversals would strengthen the correction case. These are discriminating tests, not forecasts.
