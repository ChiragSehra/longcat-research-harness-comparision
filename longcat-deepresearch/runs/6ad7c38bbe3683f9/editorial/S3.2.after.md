### S3.2｜Legislative control, constitutional arithmetic, and administrative adaptation

**The evidence supports continued ordinary legislative control alongside a consequential constitutional constraint; it does not establish that allies routinely dictated policy.** PRS recorded 179 non-financial bills passed during the 17th Lok Sabha and 16% referred to committees; by Monsoon 2026, the referral proportion in the 18th Lok Sabha was 21%. Increased scrutiny coexisted with agenda control: eleven bills passed during Monsoon 2026, nine without any Lok Sabha member other than the minister speaking. These are PRS compilations, rather than independently recovered parliamentary records. ([17th Lok Sabha assessment](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-the-17th-lok-sabha), [Monsoon 2026 assessment](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-parliament-during-monsoon-session-2026))

**Ordinary legislation—Waqf:** introduced on 8 August 2024 and referred to a joint committee, the amendment passed Lok Sabha on 2 April and Rajya Sabha on 3 April 2025, according to PRS. Both chambers debated it for approximately thirteen hours. In contrast, the simultaneous-election constitutional proposal remained listed at the committee stage. Referral therefore indicates scrutiny, not defeat. Waqf’s passage is strong contrary evidence to general opposition veto power, although it cannot establish freedom from negotiations over the final text. ([Bill chronology](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-waqf-amendment-bill-2024), [Budget Session 2025 assessment](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-budget-session-2025), [Simultaneous-election bill](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-constitution-one-hundred-and-twenty-ninth-amendment-bill-2024))

**Constitutional change—delimitation:** PRS records introduction of the three-bill package on 16 April 2026 and negation of the Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill on 17 April. The proposals would have increased Lok Sabha’s constitutional maximum to 850, enabled delimitation using the latest published census—then 2011—and removed the requirement tying women’s reservation to the first census after the 2023 amendment. These were proposals, not enacted redistribution. PRS reports 298 votes for and 230 against; the associated ordinary bills consequently became infructuous and were not considered. This demonstrates a constitutional barrier despite a numerical majority voting in favour. Without the official division list and contemporaneous party positions, it cannot establish ally defection or coalition bargaining as the cause. ([Package analysis](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-constitution-131st-amendment-bill-2026), [Budget Session 2026 assessment](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-budget-session-2026))

**Administrative and fiscal attribution remains unresolved.** PRS records Cabinet approval of caste enumeration in the upcoming census in April 2025, but that decision cannot distinguish ally pressure from broader electoral adaptation. Lateral-recruitment cancellation documents, official constitutional proceedings and substantive budget-implementation reports were not recovered. Andhra Pradesh/Bihar releases and expenditure therefore remain unverified, and announced commitments cannot be counted as delivered bargaining concessions. These gaps limit causal confidence in the finding of legislative continuity and selective constitutional constraint. ([April 2025 policy review](https://prsindia.org/policy/monthly-policy-review/april-2025))
