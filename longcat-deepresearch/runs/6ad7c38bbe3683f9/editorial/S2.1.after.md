### S2.1｜National votes, seats, and electoral conversion

**The defensible finding is a parliamentary correction, with its popular-support magnitude unresolved.** BJP’s 2019→2024 seat change was −63 (303→240), Congress’s +47 (52→99); SP won 37 seats. PRS’s ECI-based profiles corroborate these figures, although its 2019 comparison excludes the postponed Vellore election. Provisionally, BJP converted more absolute votes into fewer seats, while Congress improved conversion while contesting fewer constituencies. Electorate growth, geography, candidates and changed contest patterns prevent attributing these differences directly to coordinated vote transfer. [2019 profile](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/profile-of-the-newly-elected-17th-lok-sabha), [2024 profile](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/profile-of-the-18th-lok-sabha).

IndiaVotes’s Vellore exclusion also means DMK’s listed 23→22 seats cannot represent a complete-election comparison. Legislative mechanisms and attribution are assessed in S3.2.

**Denominator and coverage caveat:** ECI’s 26 December 2024 release records 646,420,869 EVM-plus-postal votes, including 6,371,839 NOTA votes (0.99%), and uncontested Surat. Most reports cover 542 polled constituencies; specified supplementary tables include Surat. These totals differ from the secondary database’s aggregate, preventing substitution of its shares for a consistent national series. [ECI release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2088064&reg=48&lang=2). Final party/state/constituency tables, comparable turnout and vote-share denominators, complete BSP/BJD returns and dated alliance classifications remain unrecovered. Election-date NDA/UPA/INDIA totals, fixed-group comparisons and narrow-margin coordination estimates therefore remain unresolved; earlier opposition alliances must not be retrospectively labelled INDIA.
