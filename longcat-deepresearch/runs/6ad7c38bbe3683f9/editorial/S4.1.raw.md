### S4.1｜Strongest correction case, strongest realignment case, and mixed verdict

**The strongest correction case is continuity of BJP primacy alongside reduced parliamentary autonomy.** The electoral arithmetic and dependence on partners assessed in S2.1 and S3.1 support continued predominance, although seats alone cannot establish continuity in popular support. Ordinary-law passage and continued agenda control in S3.2 strengthen this interpretation. Its principal vulnerability is that calling 2024 merely a correction understates the changed conditions for governing; continued legislative capacity also does not establish absence of ally influence.

**The strongest realignment case concerns governing constraints.** S3.2’s contrast between ordinary-law passage and constitutional defeat shows how agenda control can coexist with consequential limits on constitutional change. Attribution remains the weakness: defeat alone cannot establish ally rebellion, explain decisive votes, or show that the constraint arose specifically from 2024. Evidence of institutional constraint is therefore stronger than evidence of enduring coalition leverage.

Milan Vaishnav’s July 2024 essay offers a continuity hypothesis: coalition dependence can coexist with persistence of the political order he calls India’s “Second Republic.” Its accessible summary identifies changes in electoral democracy, constitutionalism, national identity, secularism and federalism. This remains attributed interpretation; without the full essay, its detailed reasoning cannot be assessed or substantive disagreements with Sanjay Kumar, Suhas Palshikar, Rahul Verma and Christophe Jaffrelot reconstructed. [Vaishnav, publication and summary](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/the-rise-of-indias-second-republic/).

Voting support and political inclusion are also distinct. Feyaad Allie’s April 2025 abstract argues that BJP exclusion and opposition “strategic silence” reinforced Muslim exclusion after 2024. This is an interpretation of inclusion, not a verified estimate of Muslim vote choice; the full essay was not recovered. [Allie, *India’s New Minority Politics*](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/indias-new-minority-politics/).

**The defensible adjudication is institutional change within continuing BJP predominance.** Temporary correction remains plausible but cannot yet be called one-off. Persistent voter-bloc realignment and durable regional party-system change remain unresolved given the electoral and survey gaps in S2. Parliamentary constraints have observational support, while enduring ally leverage requires documented bargaining and repeated outcomes. Neither ordinary-law passage nor constitutional defeat establishes durable changes in voter allegiance.
