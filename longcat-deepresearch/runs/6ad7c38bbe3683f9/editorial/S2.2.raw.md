### S2.2｜Regional redistribution and subsequent election tests

**Regional evidence suggests volatility and possible BJP recovery, but incomplete comparisons keep confidence low.** S2.1 supplies the national arithmetic; missing state-level parliamentary returns prevent ranking regional contributions to the 2024 change. In Uttar Pradesh and Rajasthan, unrecovered returns prevent quantifying BJP losses, comparing votes with seats, or distinguishing SP–Congress coordination from changes in BJP or BSP support. Bihar, Karnataka, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, West Bengal and the Northeast also lack adequate parliamentary comparisons. Through 11 October 2026, the subsequent election census lacks recovered direct declarations, alliance classifications and comparable denominators; secondary listings cannot certify completeness or exclude additional elections. These gaps prevent a comprehensive persistence assessment.

**Subsequent Haryana–Maharashtra strength provides provisional evidence against permanent setbacks.** The secondary assembly database records BJP winning 48 Haryana seats and 132 Maharashtra seats in 2024. Without election-date alliance totals or comparable vote denominators, these tallies demonstrate substantial electoral strength but cannot establish reversal of the Lok Sabha verdict or measure Maharashtra’s faction-specific changes. [IndiaVotes assembly index](https://www.indiavotes.com/vidhan-sabha/).

**Odisha–Andhra Pradesh offers concurrent comparisons, rather than later persistence tests.** ECI confirms that their assemblies accompanied the 2024 parliamentary election, alongside Arunachal Pradesh and Sikkim, and that statistical reports were released on 26 December 2024; its microsite gives 4 June counting dates. The secondary database cited above records BJP winning 78 Odisha assembly seats and TDP winning 135 Andhra Pradesh seats, contrasting BJP expansion with a regional party’s victory. Missing BJP/BJD parliamentary comparisons, the TDP–BJP–Jana Sena alliance total and YSRCP vote changes limit interpretation; aggregate results cannot demonstrate individual split-ticket voting. [ECI release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2088064&reg=48&lang=2), [ECI election microsite](https://elections24.eci.gov.in/).

**Disputed 2026 reports weaken a uniform opposition-realignment interpretation without supplying decisive persistence evidence.** A May report describes BJP victory in West Bengal, continued strength in Assam and TVK emerging as Tamil Nadu’s largest party. However, it contradicts itself about the Kolathur winner, while recovered sources disagree on Bengal and Tamil Nadu seat totals. Those figures remain excluded from decisive calculations; the reported outcomes cannot establish enduring voter switching.
