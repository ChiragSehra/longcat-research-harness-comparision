### S2.1｜National votes, seats, and electoral conversion

**The verified change is a substantial redistribution of parliamentary seats; the recovered evidence does not establish a comparably large national shift in popular support.** BJP fell from 303 seats in 2019 to 240 in 2024, while Congress rose from 52 to 99: calculated changes of −63 and +47. SP won 37 seats in 2024. These figures are corroborated by PRS’s ECI-based profiles; its 2019 profile covers 542 constituencies, explicitly excluding the postponed Vellore election. [2019 profile](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/profile-of-the-newly-elected-17th-lok-sabha), [2024 profile](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/profile-of-the-18th-lok-sabha).

The following comparison supplies **secondary-source observations, not reconciled final ECI returns**. Votes are rounded to millions; “contested” reproduces the publisher’s constituency counts. No vote-share or contested-seat conversion calculation is made from these unreconciled inputs. [2014 data](https://www.indiavotes.com/lok-sabha/2014/), [2019 data](https://www.indiavotes.com/lok-sabha/2019/), [2024 data](https://www.indiavotes.com/lok-sabha/2024/).

| Party | 2014: votes / won / contested | 2019: votes / won / contested | 2024: votes / won / contested |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| BJP | 171.66m / 282 / 428 | 229.08m / 303 / 436 | 235.97m / 240 / 441 |
| Congress | 106.94m / 44 / 464 | 119.62m / 52 / 422 | 136.76m / 99 / 328 |

Taken provisionally, these observations are consistent with weaker BJP seat conversion despite more absolute votes, and stronger Congress conversion while contesting fewer constituencies. They do **not** establish coordination’s causal contribution: electorate growth, geographic distribution, candidate selection and changed contest patterns must be separated before attributing the result to vote transfer.

The regional-party arithmetic also requires care. IndiaVotes reports 2019→2024 seats of SP 5→37, AITC 22→29, TDP 3→16, JD(U) 16→12, YSRCP 22→4 and AAP 1→3. Its 2019 listing excludes Vellore, so DMK’s listed 23→22 cannot be treated as a complete-election comparison. Its 2024 categories distinguish Shiv Sena (7), Shiv Sena–UBT (9), and NCP–SP (8); PRS confirms that the latter two emerged from parties represented under different labels in 2019. Combining factions would conceal alliance redistribution. [2019 data](https://www.indiavotes.com/lok-sabha/2019/), [2024 data](https://www.indiavotes.com/lok-sabha/2024/), [PRS faction caveat](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/profile-of-the-18th-lok-sabha).

Denominator reconciliation remains essential. ECI’s **26 December 2024** release records 646,420,869 EVM-plus-postal votes, 6,371,839 NOTA votes (0.99%), and an uncontested Surat seat. Most statistical reports cover 542 polled constituencies, with specified supplementary tables including Surat. These totals differ from the secondary database’s aggregate; consequently, its published shares cannot substitute for a verified, consistently defined national series. [ECI release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2088064&reg=48&lang=2).

**Evidentiary limit:** final party/state/constituency tables, comparable turnout and vote-share denominators, complete BSP/BJD returns, and dated alliance classifications were not substantively recovered. Election-date NDA/UPA/INDIA totals, fixed-group comparisons and narrow-margin coordination estimates therefore remain unresolved. Earlier opposition alliances must not be retrospectively labelled INDIA. The defensible finding is a parliamentary correction; its magnitude in popular support and its durability remain unproven by this arithmetic alone.
