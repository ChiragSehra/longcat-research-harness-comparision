### S5.1｜Unresolved evidence and discriminating future tests

**Uncertainties and limits.** Incomplete final electoral returns, unresolved later-result discrepancies and an incomplete election census limit confidence in regional persistence through 11 October 2026 (S2.1–S2.2). The [ECI elections page](https://www.eci.gov.in/elections) and [statistical-reports page](https://www.eci.gov.in/statistical-reports) yielded no substantive records; the [results portal](https://results.eci.gov.in/) yielded only a redirect. These retrieval failures do not establish that elections did not occur; schedules cannot substitute for declared results. Available secondary observations support limited comparisons, rather than a comprehensive persistence test.

Survey comparability remains unresolved because weighted tables, questionnaires and relevant later studies were not recovered (S2.3). Self-reported voting is subject to reporting error; subgroup claims require precision estimates, repeated cross-sections cannot identify individual switchers, and constituency results cannot identify caste-level movement.

Unreconciled electoral denominators and alliance classifications further limit comparisons, while assembly results cannot settle national durability. Available PRS observations support legislative continuity and selective constitutional constraint, but missing primary proceedings and budget-implementation records limit corroboration, delivery assessment and attribution to ally bargaining (S3.1–S3.2). Enduring coalition leverage therefore remains less firmly established than the change in parliamentary dependence.

Three observable tests could change the conclusion: another national election reproducing the regional pattern; comparable surveys showing sustained bloc shifts; and alliance survival accompanied by documented, repeated bargaining concessions. Until verified, these are discriminators, not forecasts.
