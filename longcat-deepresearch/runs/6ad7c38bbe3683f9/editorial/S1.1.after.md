### S1.1｜Decision and operational tests of durability

**The strongest supported answer is a consequential electoral correction with observed institutional effects; whether it was one-off or marks durable voter-bloc or regional realignment remains unresolved.** Confidence is moderate about the 2024 change, but low about persistence through 11 October 2026: missing comparable returns and weighted survey findings prevent convergence tests.

The decisive distinction is BJP versus NDA: BJP won 240 seats, below the 272 majority threshold, while NDA secured 293. Congress’s 99 seats likewise should not be conflated with INDIA’s broader strength. The result established dependence on partners at government formation while preserving BJP’s position as the largest party. [PRS’s election profile](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/profile-of-the-18th-lok-sabha) corroborates party seats; [Vaishnav and Mallory’s September 2024 analysis](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2024/09/india-election-bjp-party-politics) supplies alliance totals and the strongest continuity counterargument: BJP remained the organizing pole of competition.

Governing evidence supports selective constraints alongside continued legislative capacity. PRS records [Waqf legislation passing both chambers in April 2025](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-waqf-amendment-bill-2024), but [the constitutional delimitation bill being negatived on 17 April 2026](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-constitution-131st-amendment-bill-2026). This contrast supports institutional limits without establishing that allies caused the defeat or that coalition leverage endured.
