# ReportSpec

## Research mandate and shared evidence rules

Produce an English **1,500–2,500 word decision memo** answering: **Was India's 2024 general-election result a one-off correction or evidence of a durable political realignment?** Cover vote shares, coalition strategy, regional outcomes, major voter blocs, and governing constraints through **11 October 2026, Asia/Kolkata**. Include an executive conclusion, competing interpretations, one compact evidence table, direct source links, and a section explicitly labelled **“Uncertainties and limits.”** Do not preselect the verdict or pre-write the report.

Distinguish temporary correction, persistent voter-bloc realignment, durable regional party-system change, and enduring coalition constraints. For each proposition, record the observation, comparator, comparability limits and confounders, strongest contrary case, and resulting confidence. Repetition supports persistence only when observations are meaningfully comparable; assembly victories, seat changes, or coalition formation alone cannot establish national voter realignment. Continued BJP dominance, weaker parliamentary autonomy, and durable regional opposition gains can coexist.

Use 2014 and 2019 national elections as baselines, 2024 as the central observation, and verified subsequent elections and governing conduct as persistence tests. Prioritize final Election Commission of India (ECI) returns, original party documents, parliamentary records, and government publications. Use Lokniti-CSDS studies for individual voting patterns and signed, dated expert analysis for interpretation. Label verified facts, survey estimates, researcher calculations, party claims, attributed interpretation, and unresolved claims separately. Every material factual claim needs a direct supporting URL. Record event and publication dates; exclude evidence published after the cutoff and distinguish later publication from later events.

**Source recovery gate:** all Source Leads below remain unverified until fetched by the Researcher. Collection pages are discovery routes, not substitutes for supporting documents. Previous searches encountered HTTP 429, and an attempted June 2024 results archive returned 404; do not invent replacement archive paths. Recover accessible official tables, PDFs, questionnaires, proceedings, and divisions before calculating or drafting dependent claims. Log inaccessible evidence and seek an equivalent authoritative document; unresolved gaps must qualify the verdict.

**Ownership:** S2.1 owns vote arithmetic, margins, contest patterns, and measured coordination effects; S2.2 owns geography and election comparisons; S2.3 owns individual survey evidence; S3.1 owns agreements, organizational survival, and bargaining; S3.2 owns governing process tracing. S4 synthesizes these findings without commissioning duplicate empirical research. Each empirical unit supplies candidate evidence-table rows. The final integrator selects six to eight decisive rows with columns: indicator/date; verified observation; implication for correction; implication for realignment; principal limitation; direct source. Label calculations and derived coalition totals.

Target about 2,100 words: executive conclusion 180; electoral and voter evidence 800; coalition and governing evidence 450; competing interpretations 450; uncertainties 220, including the compact table. Keep full inventories and calculations in working notes; never create a separate Rowbook.

## S1｜Executive conclusion

### S1.1｜Decision and operational tests of durability
#### What to Cover
Write last and place first in the memo. Give the strongest supported answer, qualitative confidence, two or three decisive findings, and the strongest counterargument. Assess voter, regional, and governing durability separately before combining them.
#### Research Questions
- Which changes recur in comparable observations, which reverse, and which remain untested?
- Do votes, surveys, organization, and governing conduct converge, or does the conclusion depend mainly on seat arithmetic?
- Does a mixed regional and institutional interpretation explain the evidence better than a comprehensive national realignment?
- What observable evidence would overturn the preferred conclusion?
#### Required Entities / Cases
BJP versus NDA; Congress versus INDIA; the four propositions defined above; verified findings and contrary cases from S2–S4. Apply the common observation–comparator–confounder–counterexample rule to each proposition.
#### Source Leads
- https://www.eci.gov.in/statistical-reports — discovery of official electoral baselines; use recovered direct returns for executive factual claims.
- https://www.lokniti.org/national-election-studies — discovery of survey evidence underlying voter-persistence claims.
- https://sansad.in/ls — discovery of membership and proceedings underlying governing-constraint claims.

## S2｜Electoral change and persistence

### S2.1｜National votes, seats, and electoral conversion
#### What to Cover
Compare party votes, vote shares, seats won, seats contested, and turnout in 2014, 2019, and 2024. Separate changes in popular support from geographic seat conversion and alliance reclassification. Own constituency margins, contest patterns, and measured coordination effects; leave agreements and alliance survival to S3.1.
#### Research Questions
- What do final returns establish for major parties, including percentage-point and seat changes?
- How do coalition totals compare using both election-date membership and a consistently defined grouping? Do not retrospectively label earlier opposition alliances INDIA.
- Where did constituency swings and narrow margins turn small vote movements into substantial seat changes?
- What do contested-seat shares reveal? Specify valid-vote denominators, NOTA treatment, uncontested seats, and rounding.
- What evidence supports coordination or vote transfer without assuming perfect transfer in hypothetical counterfactuals?
#### Required Entities / Cases
BJP, Congress, SP, TMC/AITC, DMK, TDP, JD(U), BSP, AAP, BJD, YSRCP, and other materially relevant parties. Separate Sena and NCP factions using ECI identifiers. Retrieve final national, state, and constituency tables and dated alliance classifications; label all derived totals.
#### Source Leads
- https://www.eci.gov.in/statistical-reports — locate document-level 2014, 2019, and 2024 general-election returns, turnout, and constituency tables.
- https://results.eci.gov.in/ — discover accessible dated results archives and official party identifiers; corroborate provisional totals against final returns.
- https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats — discover representation summaries for cross-checking seats, not replacing ECI vote data.

### S2.2｜Regional redistribution, concurrent baselines, and subsequent elections
#### What to Cover
Rank states by contributions to BJP/NDA losses and offsetting gains. Complete the election census before selecting a few diagnostic confirming and disconfirming cases. Separate concurrent cross-level comparisons from subsequent persistence tests.
#### Research Questions
- How much of the national change originated in Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, and Haryana? Did party and alliance votes move with seats?
- What contrasting mechanisms appear in Odisha and Andhra Pradesh, and what material evidence comes from other regions?
- Were Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, Arunachal Pradesh, and Sikkim assembly contests concurrent with the 2024 parliamentary election? What do comparable aggregate results reveal about election-level effects?
- Which subsequent elections actually concluded by the cutoff, and is the census complete?
- Do later comparable vote shares persist, recover, or deteriorate after accounting for alliances, faction splits, candidates, turnout, incumbency, and election level?
#### Required Entities / Cases
Uttar Pradesh: BJP, SP–Congress, BSP; Maharashtra: both alliances and separate Sena/NCP factions; Odisha: BJP/BJD; Andhra Pradesh: TDP–BJP–Jana Sena/YSRCP. Screen Bihar, West Bengal, Karnataka, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and the Northeast for material contributions.

Verify concurrent 2024 assembly baselines in Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, Arunachal Pradesh, and Sikkim. Candidate subsequent-election census: 2024 Haryana, Jammu and Kashmir, Maharashtra, Jharkhand; 2025 Delhi and Bihar; 2026 Assam, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Puducherry. Verify occurrence, completion, and any additional early elections or reruns rather than inferring outcomes from schedules.

**Census completion gate:** working notes must record polling and declaration dates, direct result documents, party identifiers, election-date alliances, and comparable vote denominators for every completed full assembly election in scope. Reconcile schedules against declared results. Log unresolved entries before selecting cases; incomplete coverage must limit selection and confidence. Include parliamentary by-elections only under a stated diagnostic relevance rule. Aggregate cross-level differences do not establish individual split-ticket voting, and assembly outcomes are not a hypothetical Lok Sabha result.
#### Source Leads
- https://www.eci.gov.in/elections — discover schedules and notifications establishing timing and census coverage.
- https://www.eci.gov.in/statistical-reports — recover final parliamentary and assembly returns for geographic and cross-level comparisons.
- https://results.eci.gov.in/ — discover direct declared-result archives; verify actual accessible URLs.
- https://www.lokniti.org/state-election-studies — discover comparable regional surveys to evaluate mechanisms.

### S2.3｜Major voter blocs and explanations of vote choice
#### What to Cover
Assess caste, religion, gender, age, class, and rural–urban patterns using comparable surveys. Separate reported vote choice and concerns from causal explanations or durable allegiance changes. Evaluate coverage and instrument differences before making national or subgroup comparisons.
#### Research Questions
- How did BJP/NDA and opposition support vary among SC/Dalit, ST/Adivasi, OBC, upper-caste, Muslim, female, young, poorer, and rural voters?
- Where instruments and samples permit, did movement differ between Yadav and non-Yadav OBC voters, or Jatav and other Dalit voters, especially in Uttar Pradesh?
- Are national patterns driven by particular states? Do subgroup sample sizes, weighting, and design uncertainty support the claimed contrast?
- What do measured responses establish about jobs, prices, farming, welfare, leadership, reservation, and constitutional protection?
- Do later comparable studies support persistence? How do coverage, definitions, and question wording affect 2014–2019–2024 comparisons?
#### Required Entities / Cases
Lokniti-CSDS National Election Studies 2014, 2019, and 2024; weighted reports, questionnaires, fieldwork dates, method notes, and relevant subsequent state studies. **HP3:** verify the reported coverage differences: 2014, 22,295 respondents/26 states or UTs/306 constituencies; 2019, 24,236/26/208; 2024, 19,663/23/191. Assess exclusions and weighting before using these figures or comparing estimates.

Do not infer caste switching from BSP decline or other party redistribution, or treat repeated cross-sections as an individual panel. Distinguish Muslim electoral support from political inclusion only if it materially changes the bloc interpretation.
#### Source Leads
- https://www.lokniti.org/national-election-studies — **HP3:** locate coverage figures, weighted reports, instruments, and methodology for comparability checks.
- https://www.lokniti.org/state-election-studies — locate Uttar Pradesh subgroup evidence and subsequent comparable surveys.
- https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/indias-new-minority-politics/ — Feyaad Allie’s attributed argument distinguishing electoral support from inclusion; verify date and relevance, and keep its use bounded.
- https://manifesto.inc.in/ — discover campaign commitments; these cannot establish voter motivation.
- https://www.mospi.gov.in/ — discover dated economic context only where necessary; indicators cannot establish voting motives.

## S3｜Coalition organization and governing constraints

### S3.1｜Seat-sharing, alliance survival, and partner leverage
#### What to Cover
Compare election-time agreements with later organizational survival and bargaining. Test repeatable opposition cooperation and demonstrated NDA partner leverage. Use S2.1 findings for coordination effects without recalculating them here.
#### Research Questions
- Which dated membership, seat-sharing, and candidate agreements governed pivotal contests?
- Did Congress–SP cooperation and relationships with TMC, AAP, DMK, RJD, and Maharashtra partners endure when state interests diverged?
- How dependent was the Union government on TDP, JD(U), and other allies at formation and cutoff, accounting for vacancies and membership changes?
- Which demands yielded documented concessions, and what links them to bargaining rather than routine policy?
- Did portfolios, leadership changes, defections, or consolidation materially change the durability assessment?
#### Required Entities / Cases
BJP, Congress, SP, TMC, AAP, DMK, RJD, TDP/N. Chandrababu Naidu, JD(U)/Nitish Kumar, Maharashtra factions, and Andhra Pradesh/Bihar alliances. Retrieve original agreements, support statements, appointment records, and dated membership. Own bargaining evidence; S3.2 owns legislative outcomes and budget implementation.
#### Source Leads
- https://inc.in/ and https://www.bjp.org/ — discover dated original agreements and statements; retrieve equivalent regional-party documents.
- https://sansad.in/ls — discover dated membership, vacancies, and affiliations needed for dependence calculations.
- https://www.pib.gov.in/ — discover cabinet appointments and announced concessions; obtain direct dated releases.

### S3.2｜Demonstrated limits on governing freedom
#### What to Cover
Trace two or three well-documented governing episodes, comparing the preceding term and including evidence of continued agenda control. Distinguish formal dependence, special-majority arithmetic, opposition cohesion, federal disputes, and actual ally-imposed constraints.
#### Research Questions
- Which measures passed, changed, stalled, were withdrawn, or failed, and what do official divisions, committee reports, and party statements establish?
- How do chamber strength, attendance, vacancies, and ordinary versus Article 368 thresholds affect the inference?
- Do Andhra Pradesh/Bihar budget commitments establish bargaining leverage? Separate announcements, allocations, releases, expenditure, and prior obligations.
- Do Waqf legislation, simultaneous-election proposals, lateral recruitment, or caste-enumeration policy indicate constraint, continuity, or electoral adaptation?
- **HP2:** what occurred in the reported 2026 delimitation package, and did regional representation disputes, special-majority arithmetic, or ally dissent explain the outcome?
#### Required Entities / Cases
17th and 18th Lok Sabha; Rajya Sabha; recorded votes and committees; Union Budgets 2024–25, 2025–26, and 2026–27 with implementation records. Verify Waqf legislation and the Constitution (129th Amendment) proposal through cutoff.

**HP2:** verify the Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026, the associated three-bill package, reported 16 April introduction and 17 April negation, proposals concerning Lok Sabha size and census choice, and commencement of women’s reservation. Retrieve official text, proceedings, division records, and contemporaneous party positions. Separate projected redistribution from enacted change; a defeat alone does not prove coalition failure.
#### Source Leads
- https://sansad.in/ls — discover direct bill texts, proceedings, divisions, and committee records, including **HP2** corroboration.
- https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-constitution-131st-amendment-bill-2026 — **HP2:** unverified lead for package contents and chronology; corroborate consequential claims in primary records.
- https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-waqf-amendment-bill-2024 — discover chronology and linked legislative documents.
- https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-constitution-one-hundred-and-twenty-ninth-amendment-bill-2024 — discover proposal contents and subsequent status.
- https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/ — recover budget PDFs and dated records supporting allocation and implementation distinctions.

## S4｜Competing interpretations and adjudication

### S4.1｜Strongest correction case, strongest realignment case, and mixed verdict
#### What to Cover
Steelman both conclusions against the same verified evidence. Consider regional and institutional restructuring without comprehensive voter realignment. Pair each interpretation’s best evidence with its strongest vulnerability; synthesize S2–S3 rather than duplicate their research.
#### Research Questions
- Can vote continuity, concentrated losses, coordination, temporary grievances, and subsequent recoveries explain correction?
- Does realignment have repeated support in voter blocs, organizational consolidation, regional competition, or governing constraints?
- Which observations discriminate between hypotheses, and which fit both?
- **HP1:** can coalition dependence coexist with persistence of the pre-2024 political order, and what later evidence tests that argument?
- Do expert disagreements concern mechanisms, definitions, or facts? What confidence is justified for each dimension?
#### Required Entities / Cases
Correction, voter realignment, regional restructuring, and coalition constraints; the common adjudication rule and strongest contrary observations from each empirical unit.

**HP1:** Milan Vaishnav’s signed July 2024 essay arguing that a narrower victory did not dislodge deeper changes in the political settlement. Treat the argument and “Second Republic” terminology as attributed interpretation, then test against subsequent evidence. Locate contrasting signed analysis by Sanjay Kumar, Suhas Palshikar, Rahul Verma, and Christophe Jaffrelot where relevant; do not invent their positions or force equal evidentiary weight.
#### Source Leads
- https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/the-rise-of-indias-second-republic/ — **HP1:** attributed continuity argument, author, date, and supporting reasoning to verify.
- https://www.lokniti.org/ — discover actual signed analyses offering electoral and social-bloc interpretations; cite article-level URLs.
- https://carnegieendowment.org/people/milan-vaishnav — author discovery for relevant follow-up analysis, not evidence of a specific argument.

## S5｜Uncertainties and limits

### S5.1｜Unresolved evidence and discriminating future tests
#### What to Cover
Use the exact memo heading **“Uncertainties and limits.”** Explain concrete gaps and their consequences for the verdict. Separate unavailable evidence, disputed claims, measurement limits, and causal uncertainty. End with two or three observable tests that would change the assessment, without presenting forecasts as facts.
#### Research Questions
- Does the verified chronology establish whether any subsequent national general-election test occurred within the window?
- What do incomplete election coverage or inaccessible direct sources prevent the memo from concluding?
- How do survey coverage, subgroup uncertainty, self-reported voting, ecological inference, and repeated cross-sections bound switching claims?
- How sensitive are findings to alliance classifications, faction splits, seats contested, and election level?
- Can policy shifts be attributed to coalition pressure rather than electoral, legal, or administrative causes?
- If election-integrity allegations materially affect an inference, what do specific complaints, ECI responses, and adjudicated findings establish?
#### Required Entities / Cases
Actual unresolved gaps from every empirical unit; census completion status; **HP3** comparability limitations where material; source publication lag; distinction between observed persistence and durability across multiple national elections. Future discriminators may include repeat national results, comparable bloc estimates, alliance survival, and recorded bargaining. Keep integrity allegations bounded and attributed.
#### Source Leads

- https://www.eci.gov.in/statistical-reports — recover missing returns and document remaining electoral coverage gaps.
- https://www.lokniti.org/national-election-studies — retrieve method notes supporting survey limitations, including **HP3**.
