# ReportSpec

### Global Research Boundaries
- Deliver an English decision memo of 1,500–2,500 words addressing whether India’s 2024 general-election result was a one-off correction or evidence of durable political realignment. Include an executive conclusion, competing interpretations, one compact evidence table, direct source links, and a section explicitly labelled “Uncertainties and limits.”
- Evidence cutoff: 11 October 2026, Asia/Kolkata. Record publication and event dates; exclude subsequent developments. Use 2014 and 2019 as national baselines and completed elections after June 2024 as persistence tests. Do not assume any election occurred or any result from a scheduled date alone.
- Distinguish three potentially different outcomes: persistent voter-bloc shifts, a more competitive regional party system, and enduring coalition constraints on governing. Define durability through repeated observable evidence; a second national election is unavailable within this window.
- Prioritize ECI statistical reports and constituency returns; dated party documents; parliamentary divisions, committee records and bill histories; government publications; and methodologically transparent Lokniti-CSDS surveys. Use named expert analysis for interpretation, with attribution and direct links.
- Cite a direct document or result-page URL for every material factual claim. Landing pages below are discovery leads, not adequate final citations. Distinguish official measurements, survey estimates, party claims, expert interpretations, and the memo’s own inference.
- Planning limitation: seven complementary search calls failed with HTTP 429 errors. Successful page fetches identified ECI, Lokniti, Parliament, PRS, budget and Congress source collections. The Researcher must recover document-level links and conduct targeted expert and election-census searches. No substantive 2025–2026 political outcome is verified by this blueprint; dynamic homepage text requires corroboration in dated underlying records.

## S1｜Executive conclusion

### S1.1｜A calibrated answer and the tests behind it
#### What to Cover
Open with the best-supported answer, its confidence level, and the strongest counterargument. Allow a mixed conclusion: an electoral correction can coexist with lasting coalition or regional changes. Explain what additional evidence would change the judgment.
#### Research Questions
- Which definition of realignment—social allegiance, regional competition or governing dependence—best fits the verified record?
- Do independent indicators converge, or does the conclusion rely mainly on seats won in one election?
- What evidence would falsify the preferred interpretation, and which decisive test remains unavailable by the cutoff?
#### Required Entities / Cases
- BJP; Congress; NDA; INDIA; 2014, 2019 and 2024 Lok Sabha elections.
- Persistence tests: vote shares, voter blocs, alliance coordination, subsequent elections and parliamentary bargaining.
#### Source Leads
- https://www.eci.gov.in/statistical-reports — locate comparable national and state election reports.
- https://www.lokniti.org/national-election-studies — locate national surveys and methodological documentation.
- https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats — locate legislative and parliamentary-functioning comparisons.
#### Presentation
Approximately 180–220 words. State a provisional research judgment only after completing the evidence assessment; avoid a forced binary answer.

## S2｜Electoral change and its regional persistence

### S2.1｜What changed in votes, seats and electoral coordination?
#### What to Cover
Establish the national baseline and decompose seat changes into vote movement, regional concentration, alliance changes and the effects of first-past-the-post voting. Separate party support from alliance totals and electoral efficiency.
#### Research Questions
- What were BJP and Congress votes, vote shares, seats and seats contested in 2014, 2019 and 2024, using consistent ECI denominators?
- How much of the 2019–2024 seat change accompanied a change in party vote share, and where did close constituency results amplify it?
- How do alliance totals change when calculated with election-specific membership versus a consistently defined party grouping?
- Did opposition coordination reduce competing candidacies in pivotal constituencies? What can returns establish, and what requires surveys or explicit counterfactual assumptions?
#### Required Entities / Cases
- BJP, Congress, NDA and INDIA; Samajwadi Party, TMC, DMK, TDP and JD(U).
- Maharashtra’s Shiv Sena and NCP factions; consistent ECI party identifiers.
- National valid-vote denominators, turnout, constituency margins and alliance membership dates.
#### Source Leads
- https://www.eci.gov.in/statistical-reports — authoritative party, constituency and turnout data; retrieve underlying reports.
- https://results.eci.gov.in/ — discover working results archives; the previously attempted June 2024 archive URL returned 404.
- https://manifesto.inc.in/ — Congress’s 2024 campaign document and downloadable manifesto.
#### Presentation
Use approximately 220–280 words plus the memo’s compact evidence table. Include columns for indicator, baseline, 2024 change, post-election persistence, implication and direct source. Limit the table to six to eight decisive rows.

### S2.2｜Regional divergence and subsequent-election tests
#### What to Cover
Identify where the 2024 change was concentrated and where BJP or NDA gains offset losses. Test persistence using completed state elections and informative by-elections through the cutoff, recognizing that assembly and parliamentary contests answer different questions.
#### Research Questions
- How much of the national seat change came from Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan and Haryana? Which constituency patterns accompanied it?
- What countervailing evidence comes from Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Kerala, and what continuity appears in Bihar, West Bengal and Tamil Nadu?
- What is the complete census of assembly elections completed after June 2024 and before the cutoff? Verify results for Haryana, Jammu and Kashmir, Maharashtra, Jharkhand, Delhi, Bihar, Assam, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal and Puducherry where applicable.
- In each subsequent contest, did comparable party or alliance vote shares persist, recover or deteriorate? How did changed partners, turnout, candidates and state incumbency affect comparability?
- Does the record support a national trajectory, several regional trajectories, or insufficient persistence evidence?
#### Required Entities / Cases
- All states and union territories named above; BJP/NDA gains and opposition gains must both receive coverage.
- SP–Congress in Uttar Pradesh; competing Maharashtra alliances and factions; TDP–Jana Sena–BJP in Andhra Pradesh; BJD in Odisha.
- Census of completed post-2024 assembly elections; by-elections only when materially informative.
#### Source Leads
- https://www.eci.gov.in/statistical-reports — official state and constituency comparisons.
- https://results.eci.gov.in/ — locate dated state and by-election result archives.
- https://www.lokniti.org/state-election-studies — state surveys, questionnaires and available reports for mechanisms behind results.
#### Presentation
Approximately 300–380 words. Use the full election census to prevent cherry-picking, but discuss only the most diagnostic cases in prose.

## S3｜Voter blocs and the mechanisms of change

### S3.1｜Social allegiance, economic grievances and campaign effects
#### What to Cover
Assess which voter groups shifted and whether the evidence indicates enduring allegiance changes or temporary dissatisfaction. Examine overlapping identities and distinguish individual survey evidence from constituency-level inference.
#### Research Questions
- How did estimated support change between 2019 and 2024 among Dalits, Adivasis, OBC subgroups, upper castes, Muslims, women, young voters, rural voters and lower-income households?
- Which changes remain credible given sampling uncertainty, small subgroup samples, weighting and differences in survey coverage?
- What evidence connects voting to jobs, prices, agricultural concerns, welfare benefits, leadership preferences, constitutional or reservation concerns, and candidate selection?
- Do later comparable surveys support persistence? Where are repeated cross-sections being mistaken for tracking the same individuals?
- Are apparent national bloc shifts driven mainly by particular states or social subgroups?
#### Required Entities / Cases
- Lokniti-CSDS National Election Studies 2019 and 2024, including questionnaires, weighted reports and method notes.
- 2024 pre-poll and post-poll surveys; the source index lists a post-poll sample across 23 states and 191 constituencies, requiring verification in its method note.
- Dalits, Adivasis, OBC subgroups, Muslims, women, youth, rural and economically disadvantaged voters.
- Named analysis by Sanjay Kumar, Suhas Palshikar and Rahul Verma, subject to locating relevant dated publications.
#### Source Leads
- https://www.lokniti.org/national-election-studies — comparative survey datasets, sample information and method-note leads.
- https://www.lokniti.org/state-election-studies — regional checks on national subgroup interpretations.
- https://manifesto.inc.in/ — verify Congress campaign promises; promises do not establish voter motivation.
#### Presentation
Approximately 250–320 words. Attribute subgroup estimates to surveys and state material methodological limits alongside them.

## S4｜Coalition strategy and governing constraints

### S4.1｜Whether alliance changes became durable political organization
#### What to Cover
Compare NDA and INDIA coordination before the election and afterwards. Examine whether regional parties gained sustainable bargaining power and whether opposition cooperation survived contests in which partners competed.
#### Research Questions
- What were the dated alliance compositions, seat-sharing agreements and contested-seat allocations in 2019, 2024 and diagnostic subsequent elections?
- Did coordination improve electoral conversion, and how strong is the evidence for actual vote transfer between partners?
- How dependent was the Union government on TDP, JD(U) and other allies at formation and at the cutoff, accounting for membership changes and vacancies?
- Did Congress–SP coordination and INDIA relationships with TMC, AAP, DMK and regional parties endure outside the national election?
- Which documented demands, agreements or public disagreements demonstrate bargaining power, and which are merely rhetoric?
#### Required Entities / Cases
- TDP and N. Chandrababu Naidu; JD(U) and Nitish Kumar; BJP; Congress; SP; TMC; AAP; DMK.
- Maharashtra party factions and alliance agreements; Andhra Pradesh alliance.
- Government formation, ministerial allocation, dated parliamentary strength and election-specific seat sharing.
#### Source Leads
- https://sansad.in/ls — official party representation and membership records; retrieve dated records.
- https://manifesto.inc.in/ — Congress’s stated policy commitments, to compare with later alliance positioning.
- https://www.lokniti.org/state-election-studies — evidence on alliance supporters and voter transfer where available.
#### Presentation
Approximately 200–260 words. Source agreements and demands from original party documents or attributable statements; locate equivalent BJP, TDP, JD(U) and regional-party records during research.

### S4.2｜Observed limits on governing, rather than assumed coalition weakness
#### What to Cover
Test whether the electoral result changed legislation, scrutiny, spending or policy choices compared with the preceding parliamentary term. Separate formal parliamentary arithmetic from demonstrated concessions and policy reversals.
#### Research Questions
- How did majority arithmetic, Rajya Sabha support, committee scrutiny, divisions and opposition participation affect governing through the cutoff?
- Which major measures were enacted, amended, referred, delayed or withdrawn? Which changes can be traced to allied pressure rather than other political or administrative causes?
- What do budgets and implementation records show about Andhra Pradesh and Bihar commitments? Distinguish announcements, allocations and realized expenditure.
- Do candidate cases such as Waqf legislation, simultaneous-election proposals, lateral recruitment decisions and caste-enumeration policy demonstrate constraint, continuity or adaptation? Verify each case’s actual status before including it.
- Is there evidence of continuing executive agenda control that weakens the claim of a durable governing realignment?
#### Required Entities / Cases
- 18th Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha; comparison with the 17th Lok Sabha.
- Union budgets 2024–25, 2025–26 and 2026–27; Andhra Pradesh and Bihar provisions.
- Diagnostic policy cases named above, retained only if supported by dated primary records.
#### Source Leads
- https://sansad.in/ls — debates, business, committees and legislative records.
- https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats — session comparisons and legislative-functioning measures; corroborate consequential claims with parliamentary records.
- https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/ — budget speeches, expenditure statements, state transfers and implementation documentation.
#### Presentation
Approximately 250–320 words. Select two or three well-documented cases, including a case of governing continuity where evidence supports it.

## S5｜Competing interpretations

### S5.1｜The strongest correction case and the strongest realignment case
#### What to Cover
Present both explanations in their strongest evidence-based form. Evaluate a third possibility: persistent regional and coalition change without a comprehensive national voter realignment. Explain which evidence discriminates between explanations.
#### Research Questions
- Does the correction case best explain national vote continuity, geographically concentrated losses, alliance arithmetic and any subsequent recovery?
- Does the realignment case best explain repeated social-bloc movement, stronger regional opposition, durable coordination and demonstrated governing concessions?
- Which verified observations contradict each case? What would each explanation have predicted for subsequent elections and government behaviour?
- Where do named experts disagree about mechanisms or definitions rather than underlying results?
- Which explanation requires the fewest unsupported assumptions, and what confidence is warranted separately for voter, regional and governing change?
#### Required Entities / Cases
- One-off correction; durable voter realignment; regional or coalition restructuring without comprehensive national realignment.
- Sanjay Kumar, Suhas Palshikar and Rahul Verma; seek contrasting dated analysis by Milan Vaishnav or another identifiable specialist.
- Verified electoral, survey, alliance and legislative findings from S2–S4.
#### Source Leads
- https://www.lokniti.org/ — locate named researchers, publications and original survey-based analysis.
- https://www.lokniti.org/national-election-studies — empirical foundation for competing social-coalition interpretations.
- https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats — test claims about institutional consequences.
#### Presentation
Approximately 300–400 words, divided between the strongest case for each conclusion and an explicit adjudication. Expert commentary must remain attributed interpretation.

## S6｜Uncertainties and limits

### S6.1｜What the evidence cannot establish by the cutoff
#### What to Cover
Identify unresolved questions and explain their effect on the executive judgment. Separate missing evidence from evidence supporting continuity or change. Specify observable developments that would strengthen or weaken the conclusion.
#### Research Questions
- Which material claims lack accessible primary records, comparable surveys or corroboration?
- How do changing alliance membership, party splits, contested-seat denominators and national-versus-state election differences alter comparisons?
- How do survey coverage, subgroup uncertainty and ecological inference constrain conclusions about voter movement?
- Which causal claims cannot distinguish coalition pressure from electoral adaptation, economic conditions or administrative factors?
- Why can neither one national election nor a set of state elections definitively establish the next national outcome?
#### Required Entities / Cases
- Absence of a subsequent national election within the specified window.
- ECI denominator and party-identity comparability; Lokniti sampling limits; policy attribution limits.
- Outstanding evidence gaps and falsifiable persistence indicators.
#### Source Leads
- https://www.lokniti.org/national-election-studies — methodological documents and coverage limitations.
- https://www.eci.gov.in/statistical-reports — report definitions and data comparability.
- https://sansad.in/ls — distinguish documented parliamentary decisions from inferred political causes.
#### Presentation
Approximately 150–220 words. Retain the exact main-section label “Uncertainties and limits.” End with specific future tests, not a forecast presented as fact.
