# ResearchSpec Critique

The outline is structurally executable: five substantive main sections, eight H3 subsections, concrete research questions, and explicit evidence rules. It preserves the requested length, competing interpretations, compact table, cutoff, and exact “Uncertainties and limits” heading. Its main weaknesses are unresolved source access, incomplete specification of diagnostic comparisons, and overlap between electoral coordination and coalition organization. Revise before dispatching researchers; do not preselect a verdict.

Independent searches repeatedly returned HTTP 429. Browsing recovered specific expert arguments, parliamentary chronology, and survey coverage, but not an authoritative election-results census through October 2026. Consequently, neither the subsequent-election inventory nor numerical vote-share changes have been independently verified in this check. Treat these as outstanding research tasks, not confirmed omissions or outcomes.

## Actionable findings

1. **The election census needs an explicit completion gate.** S2.2 lists eleven subsequent assembly contests for verification, but available browsing did not establish their occurrence, results, or completeness. Require a working inventory recording election dates, declaration dates, direct result documents, party identifiers, alliance membership, and comparable vote denominators. Missing accessible results must qualify the verdict and case selection; otherwise the plan risks cherry-picking recoveries or reversals.

2. **Add a same-time, cross-level comparison.** S2.2 should check the 2024 assembly contests in Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, Arunachal Pradesh, and Sikkim as potential concurrent baselines, verifying their timing with ECI records. They are analytically different from subsequent persistence tests: differences between assembly and parliamentary voting during the same electoral period can reveal election-level effects. Do not classify them as post-election evidence or assume that aggregate differences demonstrate individual split-ticket voting.

3. **Turn the durability taxonomy into a common adjudication rule.** The four propositions are useful, but “repeated, comparable observations” remains underspecified. For each, require the researcher to identify the observation, comparator, confounders, and strongest contrary case. State explicitly that continued BJP dominance, weaker parliamentary autonomy, and durable regional opposition gains can coexist. Avoid interpreting every departure from 2019 as a new national party system.

4. **Resolve duplicated ownership.** S2.1 should own votes, margins, contest patterns, and measured coordination effects. S2.2 should own geography and election comparisons. S2.3 should own individual survey evidence. S3.1 should own agreements, organizational survival, and bargaining. S4 should synthesize their findings rather than commission another empirical survey. Assign the evidence table to the final integrator, with each empirical researcher supplying candidate rows.

5. **Specify subgroup contrasts without presuming their outcomes.** S2.3 should ask whether measured movement differs between Yadav and non-Yadav OBC voters, and between Jatav and other Dalit voters, especially in Uttar Pradesh, where survey instruments and samples permit. “OBC subgroups where actually measured” is too easy to overlook. Party decline or redistribution, including BSP, must not be treated as proof of particular caste voters switching.

6. **Source Leads satisfy the formal requirement but often fail the execution test.** Every H3 has entities and leads; however, repeated ECI, Lokniti, and Sansad landing pages do not identify the tables, questionnaires, proceedings, or divisions needed. The attempted June 2024 results archive returned 404. Replace broken or non-extractable leads with accessible document-level sources before calculating results. Retain collection pages only as discovery routes.

## Missing Named Methods and Source Leads

- **HP1 — Persistence of the pre-2024 political order despite coalition dependence → S4.1, informed by S3.2.** Milan Vaishnav’s signed July 2024 essay argues that narrower electoral victory did not dislodge deeper changes in the political settlement. This is a concrete missing counterargument to equating coalition government with reversal of BJP dominance. Absorb the argument as an attributed hypothesis and test subsequent evidence against it; do not adopt its “Second Republic” label as fact. [The Rise of India’s Second Republic](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/the-rise-of-indias-second-republic/).

- **HP2 — Delimitation, federal bargaining, and women’s reservation as the mechanism behind the already-listed 2026 amendment case → S3.2.** The fetched PRS page identifies the 131st Amendment as part of a three-bill delimitation package, with proposed changes to Lok Sabha size, census choice, and commencement of women’s reservation. The outline currently reduces this to an amendment defeat. Require official division records and contemporaneous party positions to distinguish regional representation disputes, special-majority arithmetic, and ally dissent. Keep projected seat redistribution separate from enacted change. [PRS bill analysis and chronology](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-constitution-131st-amendment-bill-2026).

- **HP3 — Survey coverage changes as a comparability check → S2.3.** The fetched Lokniti index reports 2024 post-poll coverage of 19,663 respondents across 23 states/UTs and 191 constituencies, versus 24,236 across 26 and 208 in 2019, and 22,295 across 26 and 306 in 2014. The draft names these studies but does not explicitly require identifying coverage differences and their effect on comparisons. Retrieve the method notes and weighted reports before claiming national or subgroup persistence. [Lokniti National Election Studies](https://www.lokniti.org/national-election-studies).

- **Additional bounded expert lead → S2.3/S4.1.** Feyaad Allie’s April 2025 essay distinguishes Muslim electoral support from political inclusion, arguing that opposition success need not improve representation. Include only if this distinction materially changes the voter-bloc interpretation; it should not expand into a separate democracy assessment. [India’s New Minority Politics](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/indias-new-minority-politics/).

No additional statistical method is necessary merely to decorate this memo. Constituency comparisons, survey comparability checks, and legislative process tracing already fit the question.

## Prioritized revision checklist

1. Resolve **HP1–HP3** in their target H3s, or explicitly reject each with an evidence-based reason.
2. Recover direct ECI returns and complete the cutoff election inventory before choosing persistence cases.
3. Add verified concurrent-election comparisons and explicit subgroup contrasts.
4. Establish subsection ownership and a common durability adjudication rule.
5. Replace generic or broken leads with document-level sources; preserve unresolved access gaps in the final uncertainty assessment.
6. Keep the final memo within its word budget: use a few diagnostic regional cases and two or three governing episodes, while retaining the full census in working notes.
